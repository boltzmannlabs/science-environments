# References: monoclonal-antibody design environment

All references below were published on or before the benchmark's 2024-09-01 knowledge cutoff. They ground the specific environment requirements shown; they do not validate the supplied dengue candidates.

1. Lefranc M-P, Pommié C, Ruiz M, et al. “IMGT unique numbering for immunoglobulin and T cell receptor variable domains and Ig superfamily V-like domains.” *Developmental & Comparative Immunology*. 2003;27(1):55–77. [PubMed 12477501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12477501/). **Supports:** declaring an antibody numbering convention and mapping framework/CDRs across chain types.
2. Zhao N, Han B, Zhao C, Xu J, Gong X. “ABAG-docking benchmark: a non-redundant structure benchmark dataset for antibody-antigen computational docking.” *Briefings in Bioinformatics*. 2024;25(2):bbae048. [PubMed 38385879](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38385879/). **Supports:** benchmarking antibody–antigen docking across varied, difficulty-labeled cases.
3. Jalali S, et al. “Evaluation of the Ability of AlphaFold to Predict the Three-Dimensional Structures of Antibodies and Epitopes.” *The Journal of Immunology*. 2023;211(10):1578–1588. [PubMed 37782047](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37782047/). **Supports:** treating antibody–antigen complex prediction as a separate and uncertain stage.
4. Lim XX, Chandramohan A, Lim XYE, et al. “Antibody Binding Modulates Conformational Exchange in Domain III of Dengue Virus E Protein.” *Journal of Virology*. 2016. [PubMed 26637461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26637461/). **Supports:** recording EDIII construct and antigen context, and avoiding assumptions that an isolated-domain epitope is accessible on a particle.
5. Crill WD, Roehrig JT. “Monoclonal antibodies that bind to domain III of dengue virus E glycoprotein are the most efficient blockers of virus adsorption to Vero cells.” *Journal of Virology*. 2001;75(16):7769–7773. [PubMed 11462053](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11462053/). **Supports:** biological rationale for studying EDIII; does not validate DV87.1 variants.
6. Dejnirattisai W, Wongwiwat W, Supasa P, et al. “A new class of highly potent, broadly neutralizing antibodies isolated from viremic patients infected with dengue virus.” *Nature Immunology*. 2015. [PubMed 25501631](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25501631/). **Supports:** keeping neutralization, serotype scope, virion-context binding, and antibody-dependent enhancement distinct from a binding prediction.
7. Raybould MIJ, Marks C, Krawczyk K, et al. “Five computational developability guidelines for therapeutic antibody profiling.” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. 2019;116(10):4025–4030. [PubMed 30765520](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30765520/). **Supports:** antibody-specific CDR-length, surface-hydrophobicity/charge, and heavy/light surface-charge descriptors as risk flags, not universal pass thresholds.
8. Mieczkowski C, Zhang X, Lee D, et al. “Blueprint for antibody biologics developability.” *mAbs*. 2023;15(1):2185924. [PubMed 36880643](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36880643/). **Supports:** separate conformational, chemical, colloidal, and interaction-related developability assessment.
9. Murray B, et al. “Identifying developability risks for clinical progression of antibodies using high-throughput in vitro and in silico approaches.” *mAbs*. 2023. [PubMed 37072706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37072706/). **Supports:** documenting model generalization limits and separating computational risk flags from measured developability.
10. Adolf-Bryfogle A, Kalyuzhniy O, Kubitz M, et al. “RosettaAntibodyDesign (RAbD): A general framework for computational antibody design.” *PLOS Computational Biology*. 2018;14(4):e1006112. [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006112](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1006112). **Supports:** structure-conditioned CDR sequence/backbone sampling and computational plus experimental evaluation of selected redesigns.
11. Luo S, Su Y, Peng X, et al. “Antigen-Specific Antibody Design and Optimization with Diffusion-Based Generative Models for Protein Structures.” *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems*. 2022. [Proceedings paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/3fa7d76a0dc1179f1e98d1bc62403756-Paper-Conference.pdf). **Supports:** antigen-complex-conditioned CDR sequence and structure generation; its input context is not antigen sequence alone.
12. Abanades B, Wong WK, Boyles F, et al. “ImmuneBuilder: Deep-Learning models for predicting the structures of immune proteins.” *Communications Biology*. 2023;6:575. [DOI 10.1038/s42003-023-04927-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-023-04927-7). **Supports:** antibody variable-domain structure prediction and residue-level uncertainty; single-antibody structure prediction is distinct from complex prediction.
13. Estep P, Reid F, Nauman C, et al. “High throughput solution-based measurement of antibody-antigen affinity and epitope binning.” *mAbs*. 2013;5(2):270–278. [PubMed 23575269](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23575269/). **Supports:** reporting affinity and epitope coverage with assay format and recognizing surface-assay limitations for some high-affinity measurements.
14. Makowski EK, Kinnunen PC, Huang J, et al. “Co-optimization of therapeutic antibody affinity and specificity using machine learning models that generalize to novel mutational space.” *Nature Communications*. 2022;13:3788. [DOI 10.1038/s41467-022-31457-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31457-3). **Supports:** treating affinity and nonspecific binding as competing objectives and validating proposed sequence rankings experimentally.
15. Movahedin M, Brooks TM, Supekar NT, et al. “Glycosylation of MUC1 influences the binding of a therapeutic antibody by altering the conformational equilibrium of the antigen.” *Glycobiology*. 2017;27(7):677–687. [PubMed 28025250](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28025250/). **Supports:** including target glycan state and construct context when they can alter epitope presentation.
16. Heckel F, Turaj AH, et al. “Agonistic CD27 antibody potency is determined by epitope-dependent receptor clustering augmented through Fc-engineering.” *Communications Biology*. 2022;5:229. [PubMed 35288635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35288635/). **Supports:** matching epitope, receptor clustering, Fc behavior, and functional assay to an agonist mechanism.
17. Dekkers G, et al. “Decoding the human immunoglobulin G-glycan repertoire reveals a spectrum of Fc-receptor- and complement-mediated-effector activities.” *Frontiers in Immunology*. 2017;8:877. [PubMed 28824618](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28824618/). **Supports:** recording Fc glycosylation/production context when Fc-mediated functions are part of the intended mechanism.
18. Bailly M, Mieczkowski C, Juan V, et al. “Predicting Antibody Developability Profiles Through Early Stage Discovery Screening.” *mAbs*. 2020;12(1):1743053. [PubMed 32249670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32249670/). **Supports:** separate biophysical measurements for self-interaction, aggregation, thermal/colloidal stability, binding, and downstream development risk.
19. Gong D, Riley TP, Bzymek KP, et al. “Rational selection of building blocks for the assembly of bispecific antibodies.” *mAbs*. 2021;13(1):1870058. [PubMed 33397191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33397191/). **Supports:** treating parental-arm selection and correct heavy/light-chain assembly as bispecific design requirements.
20. Faresjö R, Bonvicini G, Fang XT, et al. “Brain pharmacokinetics of two BBB penetrating bispecific antibodies of different size.” *Fluids and Barriers of the CNS*. 2021;18(1):26. [PubMed 34078410](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34078410/). **Supports:** combining whole-organ radioactivity, brain capillary depletion, and tissue localization when interpreting delivery and parenchymal distribution.
