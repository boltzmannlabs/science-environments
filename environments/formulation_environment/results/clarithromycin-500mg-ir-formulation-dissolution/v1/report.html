Executed benchmark report — 800 mg version

Clarithromycin 500 mg IR: Evidence-Gated Computational Formulation Screening

An executable response to the supplied benchmark using PubChem, Europe PMC, RDKit, SciPy, scikit-learn and Optuna

Read this before interpreting any number. These are 800 mg dry coated tablet designs, distinct from the earlier 750 mg session screen. No benchmark tablet was manufactured or assayed. Candidate status is DESIGN_ONLY. Candidate dissolution is NOT_PREDICTED; stability, compatibility and manufacturability remain UNKNOWN. Curves in this report are published comparator data or descriptive fits to those data, not predictions for the new recipes. The three proposed designs are unrankable on efficacy.

1. Objective, decisions and what was completed

The benchmark requests at least ten unique compositions containing exactly 500 mg clarithromycin and only the eight named excipients, with mass balance and concentration bounds enforced. It explicitly permits an honest fallback to three varied designs for testing when trained, applicable predictive models are absent. That fallback is the scientifically defensible outcome here.

This execution checked all twelve supplied designs, generated twelve additional compositions with seeded Latin hypercube sampling, rejected ten deliberate negative controls, retrieved source evidence, calculated molecular descriptors, refitted published release means and selected three matched-contrast designs using an information criterion. All twenty-four valid recipes remain available; no poor performance is inferred for unselected candidates.

The design namespace is clarithromycin_800mg_benchmark_v1. F01–F12 preserve the attached document's identifiers; they must not be confused with same-named formulations in the earlier 750 mg project. L01–L12 are additional computational designs. The attached original was read but not modified; its SHA-256 is recorded in verification.json.

The core comprises API, MCC, crospovidone, PVP K30, silica and magnesium stearate. Coat solids comprise hypromellose, titanium dioxide and PEG 4000. The computational process stratum is PVP K30 wet granulation, with provisional 50:50 intragranular/extragranular crospovidone allocation, MCC PH102 and a low-viscosity film-coating grade of HPMC. A production charge would require a defined potency/moisture convention. If the API is not 100% on the chosen as-is basis, calculate the mass needed for 500 mg active-equivalent and revalidate MCC, totals and bounds; do not label an uncorrected physical charge as exactly 500 mg active.

The validator enforces the exact ingredient identities, finite nonnegative masses, 500 mg API, fixed 800 mg total, 100 ±0.1% mass balance, ingredient bounds, core–coat conservation and uniqueness. The fixed 800 mg target is checked more tightly than the percentage tolerance so that a small total-mass error is not silently accepted. Near duplicates are rejected if every non-API coordinate differs by less than 1 mg from an existing recipe. The observed minimum pairwise maximum-coordinate difference is 2 mg.

All three have a 768 mg dry core and 32 mg coat solids. PEG/HPMC and TiO2/HPMC are each 0.5 by mass; this is a practical film-formation question, not proof that the coating is acceptable. Regulatory acceptability, particularly TiO2 in the intended market, needs independent assessment.

F02 — common anchor

F02 provides the central comparison for the binder and disintegrant contrasts: 35 mg crospovidone and 24 mg PVP. It is not demonstrated to have the best release, lowest degradation or best manufacturing window. The retained baseline is a design convenience, not a performance optimum.

F03 — higher-disintegrant contrast

F03 replaces 10 mg MCC with crospovidone, leaving binder, glidant, lubricant and coat fixed. It tests whether increased disintegration support improves penetration and intact-API release, while examining compactability and moisture sensitivity. Solubility, gelation or phase transition may defeat the simple “more disintegrant means better dissolution” hypothesis.

F04 — lower-binder contrast

F04 reduces PVP by 8 mg and replaces that mass with MCC. It tests water-penetration versus granule/compact strength and friability. Lower binder is not assumed to improve compatibility or shelf-life. Because these are mixtures, the MCC substitution is part of each contrast; it is not possible to interpret the change as a pure isolated ingredient effect.

Endpoint status for every finalist: D10/D15/D30/D45/D60, t50/t80, f2, full predicted profile and 90/95% predictive intervals are unavailable. No compatibility pass, manufacturing pass, degradation probability or efficacy rank is assigned. The candidate JSON follows the supplied schema with explicit nulls and risk flags.

5. Optimization that is justified without response data

The illustrative endpoint weights in the benchmark—40% dissolution, 25% stability, 20% manufacture, 10% compatibility and 5% confidence—were not applied. Filling unknown endpoints with favorable values, renormalizing around missing evidence or drawing a pharmaceutical Pareto front would create an unsupported quality ranking.

Instead, Optuna's seeded TPE sampler ran 100 trials on a narrowly defined experimental-design question: retain F02 as an anchor and select two additional designs from F01/F03/F04/F05 to distinguish two main effects. These five recipes hold all other components except balancing MCC constant. Other recipes are excluded from this particular objective because they change additional variables, not because they are predicted to perform worse.

For each selected recipe: x = [1, (CP_mg − 35)/32, (PVP_mg − 24)/24].
Choose the two contrasts maximizing det(XᵀX), where X contains the F02 anchor and the two contrasts.

F03/F04 was selected using the declared mechanistic preference to probe higher crospovidone and lower binder. F01/F04, F01/F05 and F03/F05 are equally optimal under the information geometry. Thus even this objective does not uniquely establish a “best” formulation set.

The chosen three-point design has rank three for an intercept and two main effects, but zero residual degrees of freedom at the formulation-design level. It cannot estimate interaction, curvature or lack of fit. Independent repeat batches are necessary for error estimation, and broader mixture/process designs are needed later. The determinant assumes equally informative, independent response errors; it has no direct pharmaceutical response units.

R2 establishes an important API–medium mechanism: carboxylates such as citrate can produce a surface gel that suppresses solvent penetration and tablet disintegration. This is not a demonstrated incompatibility with crospovidone or PVP. R3 shows that form I can dissolve faster intrinsically yet release more slowly from tablets because solution exposure creates fine surface form IV crystals. A molecular solubility descriptor therefore cannot determine tablet dissolution. A1 adds an acidic-medium complication: surface gel can slow penetration while dissolved clarithromycin decomposes. A low intact-API signal can reflect coupled release and degradation rather than simple slow dissolution.

R1 is the nearest practical IR comparator but uses croscarmellose, starch, talc and a different coating system, with coated weights around 875 mg. Its procedure reports six dissolution vessels and a 30-minute sample, while its tables also report 5, 10, 15 and 20 minutes; the complete sampling/correction protocol is not documented sufficiently to reproduce the experiment. These mismatches and absent unit-level observations preclude training a credible exact-recipe model from these tables.

DailyMed's 500 mg label confirms qualitative use of several named ingredients but also lists additional, different ingredients. It discloses no quantitative eight-component formula. The FDA Q&A explicitly says dissolution methods and acceptance criteria should be product-specific. The official database overview was retrieved, but no applicable clarithromycin-specific QC row or licensed USP criterion was established in this run.

7. Published profile reanalysis with SciPy: useful diagnostics, not new predictions

Seventeen source HTML tables were archived. The dissolution tables yielded sixteen profile records and eighty published mean/SD timepoints; the same innovator curve appears in both tables and is preserved with separate provenance, not counted as independent experimental evidence. No synthetic individual replicates were generated, and release values above 100% were retained as reported rather than clipped.

For every published profile, arithmetic f1 and f2 were recalculated against the reported reference using all five mean timepoints. The standard eligibility conditions were not established: multiple points exceed 85%, individual profiles and suitable unit counts are absent, and reference identity/batch details are limited. These calculations are descriptive and are not declared regulatory similarity passes.

For R1 OF7, first-order in-sample RMSE is 2.6237 percentage points and Weibull RMSE is 0.9869. The lower residual does not demonstrate external predictive superiority. There are only five mean timepoints; the Weibull fit has two residual degrees of freedom, and its AICc is undefined when the residual-variance parameter is included. The R1 stability tables also warn against reducing stability to one late release value: its reported OF7 five-minute mean changes from 70.83% initially to 61.66% at month three, a 9.17-percentage-point decrease, while its 30-minute month-three mean is 99.73%. The stability table uses a different reference curve from the initial profile comparison, so those reference records must not be pooled. There is therefore no mean/linear/random-forest response benchmark, formulation-grouped cross-validation, independent held-out batch evaluation, calibrated coverage or trained applicability-domain assessment to report. scikit-learn was used only for bound-scaled feature geometry and distances. “Inside the assumed concentration bounds” must not be relabeled “inside a validated model domain.”

8A. Follow-up: executing the PTCalc dissolution library

Following the request to use dissolution tools directly, PTCalc.Core 1.1.0 was downloaded from the cited Zenodo archive and its published MD5 checksum verified. A Microsoft .NET SDK 10.0.404 was installed in local Sci profile scratch space after verifying the official SHA-512. No system-wide SDK or package installation was made. A C# harness referenced the unmodified upstream library; MathNet.Numerics 5.0.0 was restored locally. Every upstream source file was subsequently compared byte-for-byte with the archived source.

The harness actually executed sixteen catalog models on each of the sixteen published profile records, plus First-order(Fmax) and Weibull(Fmax) variants. This produced 288 fit records: 256 catalog fits and 32 additional free-asymptote fits. The library flagged all as converged and emitted no fit exceptions. Its FitAll list contains fifteen ranked models per profile; the default slab Hopfenberg direct fit is retained separately. All raw fit parameters, warnings, secondary times, fitted grids, goodness-of-fit values and raw ranks are saved in ptcalc_run/results/ptcalc_model_fits.json. Reviewed flat exports for all 288 records are in ptcalc_all_fits_reviewed.csv/json; these are not omitted just because only OF7 is displayed below.

PTCalc calculated f1/f2 and model-independent AUC, dissolution efficiency and truncated mean dissolution time. Independent Python calculations matched all sixteen profile summaries to a maximum difference of 1.42e-14; observed-time predictions and residual sums of squares were checked for every fit. Across the 32 matched free-asymptote fits, the maximum observed-time prediction difference between SciPy and PTCalc was 0.00008117 percentage points. These comparisons test numerical implementation agreement, not transfer to an unseen tablet formulation.

The raw AIC leader for OF7 is Logistic(Fmax), but the library warns that its fitted plateau exceeds 100%. Makoid–Banakar can decline after a fitted peak, another physical caveat. A low residual or high Akaike weight does not identify a true release mechanism, and every scientific warning is preserved in the full export.

The displayed Weibull coefficient list contains derived Td in addition to fitted coefficients. Td is not an extra optimized parameter: the base Weibull has two fitted parameters and Weibull(Fmax) has three. PTCalc uses α=tau^β, whereas the SciPy implementation used tau directly. PTCalc secondary T50/T80 target absolute percentages of label claim; the prior SciPy fields explicitly targeted fractions of the fitted asymptote, so those times are not directly interchangeable. With a free plateau, Td is a Weibull scale time, not necessarily the time to 63.2% of label claim. After the parameterization conversion, the fitted mean curves agree closely.

PTCalc reports residual RMSE=sqrt(SS/(n−p)), while the earlier SciPy tables report mean RMSE=sqrt(SS/n). Its information criteria also use a different convention: AIC=n·ln(SS)+2p and AICc=AIC+2p(p+1)/(n−p−1), with p counting curve parameters, not the residual-variance parameter. The earlier SciPy report explicitly counted variance. AIC values can differ by constants and AICc corrections are not interchangeable. This follow-up preserves both definitions instead of silently relabeling them.

11. Executable follow-up experimental plan

Before execution, fix API assay/form/PSD, grades, route settings, batch size, packaging, method version and product-specific acceptance criteria. Obtain scientific approval of the benchmark bounds and coating ratios and the jurisdictional TiO2 assessment. Use XRPD, PSD, KF and assay for raw material control. Study API-only, binary and targeted multicomponent mixtures under justified thermal/humidity conditions using stability-indicating chromatography, with DSC/FTIR/XRPD as supporting rather than conclusive compatibility tests.

For manufacture, measure granule PSD, moisture, bulk/tapped density, flow/shear behavior and blend uniformity, then compression/ejection profiles, porosity, tensile strength, friability, weight/content uniformity and disintegration. Compare coated with uncoated cores and inspect coat adhesion, defects and weight gain. Predefine process windows instead of treating nominal composition as proof of manufacturability.

The generated plan contains 9 proposed independent batches: B800-F02-B01 through B03, B800-F03-B01 through B03 and B800-F04-B01 through B03. An exploratory allocation of six units per batch in a primary method and a pH-matched citrate challenge would require 108 units and 756 planned timepoint rows at 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 and 60 minutes. These are blank, explicitly PLANNED_NOT_RUN records—not simulated results. The exact medium, pH, agitation, volume and method remain approval prerequisites. Six-unit exploration is not a substitute for an appropriately designed formal profile-similarity study.

Establish specificity for intact API and degradants, sink and solution stability, filter recovery and sampling-volume corrections. Compare scientifically justified carboxylate and non-carboxylate conditions while controlling pH and ionic strength where possible. Do not attribute every release change to excipient choice. Investigate API solid form before and after exposure to distinguish crystal-transition effects from disintegrant failure.

Perform packaged long-term and accelerated stability under market-appropriate conditions; 40°C/75% RH and 25°C/60% RH are examples only, not automatically the applicable program. Track assay, individual/total impurities, moisture, appearance, dissolution and mechanical behavior. Reject candidates failing pre-agreed quality gates. Conditional D30≥80%, disintegration≤15 minutes, friability≤1% and f2≥50 in the attachment are illustrative screening suggestions, not automatically binding release criteria.

With actual formulation/batch data, compare simple mean and linear baselines with flexible surrogates, split by formulation/batch to prevent leakage, report MAE/RMSE and interval coverage on an independent set, and define domain limits. Use BoTorch or a constrained multiobjective optimizer only once meaningful measured response vectors and uncertainty exist. Repeat selected formulations to estimate error, and expand the design to estimate interactions/curvature before claiming robust optimization.

13. Conclusions

The computational benchmark is complete at the composition-artifact level: twenty-four unique feasible recipes, ten validated rejection controls and three evidence-motivated test designs. A restricted experimental-information objective was genuinely optimized and independently checked, but no pharmaceutical performance objective was estimated. Published profile reanalysis exposed both mechanism risks and a numerical source inconsistency, illustrating why literature numbers require provenance and eligibility checks.

The scientific next step is physical evidence, not a more elaborate unsupported recipe score. Until method-matched experimental observations and an externally validated model exist, the truthful conclusion is: F02, F03 and F04 are proposed experiments; all are unrankable on efficacy, with dissolution NOT_PREDICTED and other performance endpoints UNKNOWN.
