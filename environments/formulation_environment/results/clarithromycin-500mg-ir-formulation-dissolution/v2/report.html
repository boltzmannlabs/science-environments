Executed improvement study — version 2

Do the Proposed Changes Improve the Clarithromycin Dissolution Analysis?

A prospective computational comparison, an expanded interaction-capable formulation design, and an explicit separation from laboratory evidence

Scope. The computational changes below were actually run. No new formulation was manufactured, and no API, excipient, assay or stability measurement was created. Results concern reconstruction of held-out timepoints within published comparator curves. They are not predictions for F02/F03/F04 or the new corner recipes.

1. Direct answer

Some changes improved selected diagnostic errors, but there was no universal improvement. Capping Weibull’s asymptote at 100% reduced its held-out 30-minute RMSE from 3.3595 to 2.0197 percentage points (39.88% reduction). However, the same cap worsened overall leave-one-timepoint-out RMSE. The study therefore supports more careful validation and experimental design—not a claim of better tablet dissolution.

A simpler first-order model fixed at 100% had the lowest observed forward-30 RMSE among the tested configurations: 1.6032 percentage points, compared with 1.8817 for the earlier free-plateau logistic family (14.80% lower). This is an exploratory comparison on one literature study, not an independently confirmed model-selection result. The unconstrained logistic remained the lowest-error model in the broader LOTO comparison.

2. Prespecified computational changes

The previous 800 mg benchmark was retained unchanged. A separate v2 project froze eleven configurations before evaluation: mean and linear-interpolation baselines; free-plateau and ≤100%-plateau first-order, Weibull and time-logistic fits; fixed-100% first-order and Weibull fits; and a zero-origin, capped logistic variant. Nonlinear fits used three deterministic starting points, bounded least squares and the lowest successful residual sum of squares. Rates and shapes were positive; a free plateau was bounded at 150%, a cap at 100%. These numerical limits and all model equations are in apply_improvements.py.

The free Weibull is A[1−exp(−(t/τ)^β)]; the first-order is A[1−exp(−kt)]; the time-logistic is A·sigmoid(k(t−g)). The origin-corrected logistic subtracts its zero-time value and rescales to its plateau. These variants are not assumed to describe the true mechanism. The unconstrained time-logistic permits a nonzero mathematical intercept; that is a physical extrapolation caveat, not a measured burst.

One repeated reference profile was excluded from aggregate evaluation after matching its source identity and exact times/means. Its provenance remains recorded. Fifteen distinct published profiles were evaluated, but they still come from one article and are not fifteen independent external studies. Source values above 100% were not clipped, and no final-point normalization was used. Source-reported SD was not converted into invented unit-level observations or calibrated intervals.

3. Validation rather than fitting alone

For each model/profile, three views were calculated: fitting all five means; leaving out each of the five timepoints in turn; and fitting through 20 minutes before evaluating the withheld 30-minute mean. The forward-30 diagnostic is exactly the 30-minute fold of LOTO, not a second independent validation dataset. Across configurations the run contains 1,155 profile/model/split evaluations and 1,815 fitted-or-held-out point records.

LOTO at 10, 15 and 20 minutes assesses interpolation within the same curve; omitting 5 or 30 minutes requires boundary extrapolation. Even though the test point is excluded from parameter fitting, the remaining observations belong to the same published formulation and analytical system. This is not formulation-grouped or batch-grouped external validation. Selecting the best configuration after this comparison remains exploratory and requires independent confirmation.

The first-order cap modestly improved LOTO error. Weibull and logistic caps worsened aggregate LOTO RMSE even though some individual profiles improved. Weibull’s forward-30 improvement was much larger than logistic’s, while first-order’s free-versus-cap forward difference was negligible. These mixed outcomes rule out a blanket statement that physical constraints improved every result.

A bounded curve cannot reproduce a genuinely reported value above 100% exactly. Such a mismatch may reflect label-claim normalization, content variation or analytical issues; the data cannot distinguish those causes here. Physical bounds are a sensitivity analysis until assay/content reconciliation is available. The observed lowest forward error of the fixed-100 first-order model does not prove that 100% is the correct asymptote for every product or that the mechanism is first order.

6. Apply the experimental-design improvement

The earlier F02/F03/F04 set was saturated for only an intercept and two main effects. The new design fills all combinations of crospovidone 25/35/45 mg and PVP 16/24/32 mg while holding all other non-MCC components fixed. MCC balances mass. Five compositions already existed in the 800 mg benchmark; four new corner recipes complete the grid. The expanded overall pool contains 28 unique, constraint-valid recipes.

The nine-point matrix has full rank for an intercept, two linear effects, their interaction and two quadratic terms. With two additional independent center batches, the proposed eleven-batch pilot has two pure-error and three lack-of-fit degrees of freedom. These are planned algebraic degrees of freedom, not observed error estimates. Replication is initially concentrated at the center; subsequent confirmation must repeat finalists and assess relevant process, material and scale variation.

This design supports learning interactions that the old three-point set could not identify. It does not establish that any new corner is superior or that one should replace the previous finalists before data are collected. All 28 recipes remain DESIGN_ONLY and all recipe-level dissolution values remain NOT_PREDICTED.

7. Apply method and sampling improvements without inventing measurements

An investigational method draft records the R1 comparator conditions—USP II, 50 rpm, 900 mL acetate pH 5.0 and 37±0.5°C—as a development comparator only, not a verified QC method. It adds citrate and non-carboxylate challenges with pH/ionic strength to be justified. Acetate is itself a carboxylate, so its medium-specific behavior must be assessed rather than assumed benign. Exact challenge compositions, reference identity and acceptance criteria remain prerequisites requiring approval.

The planned sampling schedule is 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 and 60 minutes. The first three times are newly proposed observations, not values generated from curve interpolation. Eleven proposed batches across three media and six exploratory units per batch/medium correspond to 198 units and 1,980 blank timepoint records. Every row is labeled PLANNED_NOT_RUN, with measured API/degradant fields empty. This is not a claim that such a study has been conducted, nor a substitute for a justified formal similarity design.

The prospective gate rejects Korsmeyer–Peppas mechanism interpretation unless eligible early observations exist; it does not allow PTCalc’s all-point fallback to masquerade as early-release evidence. No t50, t80, D45 or D60 is supplied for the new recipes. No shelf-life, manufacturing window or compatibility result is inferred.

8. Evidence, uncertainty and remaining limitations

All response data remain R1 published comparator means and SD, not raw unit observations. The article used different excipients and coatings and a different tablet mass; its API form/PSD and process cannot be treated as matched to this recipe set. The mechanism references remain relevant challenge evidence: carboxylate surface gelation (Inukai et al., DOI 10.1016/j.ijpharm.2017.01.065), polymorph-transition-related suppression (Fujiki et al., DOI 10.1002/jps.24516), and coupled acidic-medium release/degradation (DOI 10.1248/cpb.59.553). They do not quantify the performance of these new recipes.

Calibrated 90/95% prediction intervals are unavailable. Five correlated mean timepoints per profile, one source study and unknown material/process differences do not justify candidate-level interval coverage. No randomized timepoint split is represented as independent batch validation, and no p-values or inferential confidence statements are used to overstate the paired diagnostic differences.

The next causal test requires material characterization, method qualification, actual batch manufacture and assay. Approval of bounds, coating ratios, market-specific TiO2 acceptability and the potency convention is still needed. No scientific gate is silently passed because a field is missing.

9. Verification and reproducibility

The run validates all new recipes against the unchanged eight-excipient bounds, fixed API dose, finished mass, core/coat conservation and the declared 1 mg near-duplicate rule. Source hashes establish that the earlier candidate and published-profile files were not modified. Model outputs, optimizer parameters, every split, all paired results and planned data templates are retained in this directory.

Execute /usr/bin/python3 apply_improvements.py to reproduce the calculations, then build_improvement_report.py and convert_report_docx.py for the report. The environment uses the same NumPy/pandas/SciPy stack as the previous benchmark. The study uses SciPy for the new constrained/held-out fits; the earlier actual PTCalc results are preserved as a baseline and were not relabeled as a new laboratory experiment.

10. Conclusion

The changes improved evaluation integrity and made the proposed experiment capable of estimating binder–disintegrant interaction and curvature. Selected forward-time errors improved, while other held-out errors worsened. The appropriate conclusion is a conditional computational improvement, not universally better predictions and not improved tablet performance. The comparison also shows why a small fitted-versus-literature difference is not enough to choose a formulation.

The data-dependent lowest-error model should be taken forward only as a candidate for external testing. The real formulation improvement question remains unanswered until matched, traceable laboratory data are available.
