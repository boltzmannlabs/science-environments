Differential abundance report (Control vs Treated)
===============================================

Proteins tested: 500; replicates per condition: 3
Prior variance s0^2 (robust median of per-protein residual variances): 0.08444
Prior degrees of freedom df0: fixed at 10 (moderate shrinkage, dfmod=14)
Moderated df per protein: 14.0

Data characteristics:
- Within-group residual variance spread: min 0.0007875, median 0.08444, max 12.74 (ratio ~151x).
  Strong heteroscedasticity justifies variance moderation rather than a plain t-test.
- log2 fold-change range: -4.026 to 2.391; median 0.015.

Why this test:
- With only 3 replicates per condition, each protein has df=4 and its
  variance estimate is noisy, so per-protein p-values are unstable and
  conservative (low effective df).
- The empirical-Bayes moderated t-test borrows information across all
  proteins to stabilize the variance and raises the effective df (14),
  improving ranking and power while still respecting each protein's own
  variance.
- The prior variance is the robust median (not the mean) because the data
  is strongly heteroscedastic; the mean would be dominated by a few
  high-variance proteins and overstate the variance for most proteins.
- Benjamini-Hochberg controls the false discovery rate at the stated cutoff.

Results:
- Significant (adj_p < 0.05): 44 (25 up, 19 down).
- Unadjusted p < 0.05: 82.

Caveats:
- n=3 per condition limits power; small true changes may be missed and the
  FDR estimate has wide uncertainty.
- No fold-change filter was applied, so some calls may have small effect
  sizes; the moderated test + BH FDR is the sole significance criterion.
- The prior df0 is a fixed modelling choice (10); it does not change the
  log2fc estimates, only the p-values/ranking.
