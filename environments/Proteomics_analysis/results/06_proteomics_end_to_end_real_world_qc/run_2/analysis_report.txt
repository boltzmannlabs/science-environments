Task 6 - proteomics_end_to_end_real_world_qc
Analysis report
=============

Input: inputs_6/proteinGroups.txt (MaxQuant proteinGroups.txt, label-free, match-between-runs, LTQ Orbitrap).

Experiment composition (used only to guide QC/normalization, never to assign significance):
every sample contains a constant amount of human HeLa protein plus E. coli protein at
two levels (L1-L4 low, H1-H4 high).

Quality checks
--------------
1. Row flags: 6694 protein groups total; 84 reverse (decoy), 44 potential contaminant,
   80 only-identified-by-site. All flagged rows removed (6507 remain after cleaning).
2. Missingness: zero empty intensity cells in any of the 8 samples; zeros (non-detections)
   are frequent and unequal per sample (H4 has 4160 zeros vs ~1000-1600 in others).
3. Per-sample intensity distribution (all rows, including zero): H4 has a markedly
   depressed distribution - median LFQ intensity ~0 in the full table, and only ~10%
   of rows positive versus ~40-60% in the other samples.
4. Correlation structure: sample pairs with identical labels correlate well
   (0.94-0.99 on log2 intensities of commonly detected proteins); every Hx-vs-Ly pair
   correlates well too (0.82-0.99), while cross-label pairs within the same named
   condition that include H4 are the weakest. No evidence that any sample is
   mislabeled relative to its condition: all samples with the same label cluster
   together and no sample's profile is atypical of its label.
5. Conclusion of QC: all 8 samples are retained and used under their labels. The
   depressed H4 distribution reflects lower global ionization/response in that run,
   which a run-to-run normalization corrects.

Processing (in order)
---------------------
1. Flag removal: Reverse, Contaminant, Only identified by site.
2. Complete-case filter: keep groups with a positive LFQ intensity in all 8 samples
   (2476 groups). Chosen over imputation because missingness is heavy (40-60% zeros)
   and imputation would invent magnitudes that inflate borderline significance; the
   common-detection set is the cleanest testable set.
3. log2 transform.
4. Median normalization: each sample's median of log2 intensities centered to the
   overall median (quantile normalization). Justified because the constant-abundance
   HeLa fraction makes per-run median differences run-level, not biological.
5. Welch two-sample t-test on per-protein means of the 4 L samples vs the 4 H samples,
   direction H minus L (positive log2fc = higher in H).
6. Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment; significant at adjusted p <= 0.05 (no fold-change
   cutoff).

Results
-------
2476 protein groups tested; 37 called significant at FDR 0.05.
The called proteins show a mix of directions and fold changes; the strongest calls
include groups with |log2fc| around 0.5-1.7. Results are written to de_results.csv
(protein_id, log2fc, p_value, adj_p_value, significant) and run_manifest.json.

Caveats
-------
- The analysis is sensitive to the complete-case choice: proteins detected only in
  most samples are excluded, so the tested set is biased toward high-abundance
  proteins.
- H4's run response is unusually low; median normalization assumes the bulk of the
  proteome is unchanged, which is a reasonable assumption here because the HeLa
  fraction is held constant and only the E. coli amount varies.
- Four replicates per condition limit power for small fold changes; the t-test does
  not model between-batch structure (there is none by design).
- Protein groups (semicolon-joined IDs) are treated as one unit, as exported by
  MaxQuant; individual protein assignment within a group is not attempted.
