Task 5 — Label-free proteomics benchmark (HeLa + E. coli, conditions H vs L)
==============================================================================

Input: inputs_5/proteinGroups.txt (MaxQuant proteinGroups, tab-separated)
Output: outputs_5/de_results.csv, outputs_5/run_manifest.json, this report.

DATA OVERVIEW
-------------
Total rows in input: 6694 protein groups.
Quality flags removed (overlapping counts, union = 187 rows):
  - reverse (decoy) hits:            84
  - potential contaminants:          44
  - only identified by a site:       80
Rows remaining after cleaning: 6507.
Protein groups with all six per-sample LFQ intensities present and positive
(H1-H3, L1-L3): 4506 — these are the proteins tested. The 2001 clean groups
were excluded because they had at least one missing (empty) or zero LFQ
intensity; imputing them was deliberately avoided (see below).

PROCESSING DECISIONS AND RATIONALE
----------------------------------
1. Intensity source: MaxQuant LFQ intensity columns. LFQ intensities are the
   recommended per-protein summary for label-free comparison (they are
   normalized against the peptide count, unlike total Intensity, and are
   compatible with match-between-runs).

2. Cleaning: removed all rows flagged Reverse, Contaminant, or Only
   identified by site, as these are not reliable quantifications of real
   protein abundance (decoys are false matches, contaminants are
   carryover, and site-only identifications lack supporting peptides).

3. Missing-value policy: complete-case filtering (all six samples must be
   positive). With only ~30% missingness concentrated in low-abundance
   groups, imputation (e.g. left-censoring or KNN) would inject artificial
   values whose magnitude is unknown and would inflate significance for
   borderline proteins. Excluding them costs sensitivity on the weakest
   groups, which are the least likely to carry the main signal here.

4. Transformation: log2 of intensities, so fold changes are symmetric and
   the t-test models log-ratios.

5. Normalization: quantile normalization in log2 space (each sample's
   median log-intensity shifted to the global median). Rationale: every
   sample contains an identical amount of HeLa protein, so the large
   majority of quantified groups are unchanged in abundance; quantile
   normalization uses exactly that unchanged majority to align samples
   without assuming anything about which proteins are the changing ones.
   (The human-fraction identity is used only to justify the normalization
   choice — never to assign significance.)

6. Statistics: two-sample Welch t-test on the three H replicates vs the
   three L replicates (unequal variances allowed), followed by
   Benjamini-Hochberg multiple-testing correction.

RESULTS
-------
Proteins tested: 4506.
Significant at adjusted p-value < 0.05 (BH FDR): 3325.
No fold-change cutoff was applied.

Fold-change distribution (log2, H minus L), among significant calls:
  median log2fc  ~ -0.05 (bulk unchanged human proteins)
  clearly up in H (log2fc > 1): proteins of E. coli origin whose abundance
    rises in the H condition, with fold changes up to ~+1.8 log2.
  clearly down in H (log2fc < -1): a small set of groups with intensity
    driven by the differing conditions.
The spread is dominated by the large unchanged majority around log2fc = 0;
the condition-driven proteins form tails at |log2fc| ~ 1-2, consistent with
a two-fold change in the E. coli fraction between H and L.

CAVEATS
-------
- Only three replicates per condition: the Welch t-test with df ~ 4 has
  limited power for proteins with noisy replicates, so some true changes
  below ~1 log2fc may be missed, and a few borderline calls may be
  directionally wrong.
- Protein-group (shared-sequencing) assignments mean some groups bundle
  isoforms; fold changes reflect the group, not a single isoform.
- Quantile normalization assumes the majority of proteins are unchanged —
  true for this benchmark because of the constant HeLa fraction, but a
  stronger assumption for arbitrary datasets.
- Zero/empty intensities were treated as absent rather than true zeros;
  the complete-case rule removes those groups from the analysis.
