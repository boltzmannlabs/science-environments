Task 4 — Protein Quantification from Peptide Intensities
=========================================================

1. INPUT DATA SUMMARY
---------------------
- Total peptide rows: 2,525
- Peptides matching exactly one protein (unique): 2,338
- Peptides matching more than one protein (shared): 187
- Proteins with at least one unique peptide: 300
- Peptides per protein: min 3, median 8, max 12
- Missing values per sample: ~370–424 out of 2,525 (~15–17%)
- Intensity range: linear scale, from ~1e5 to ~5e9

2. WITHIN-PROTEIN PEPTIDE CONSISTENCY
-------------------------------------
- Mean max-min spread of log2 intensities across peptides of the same
  protein in the same sample: ~3.7 log2 units (median 3.69).
  This means peptides from the same protein can differ by up to
  ~13x in linear intensity even in the same sample, confirming the
  task's note that peptides ionize differently.
- ~55% of peptides fall within 1 MAD of their protein-sample median,
  as expected for a symmetric distribution.
- The high within-protein spread argues against using a simple mean:
  a single outlier peptide (high ionization efficiency) would pull
  the mean upward.

3. METHOD CHOICE
----------------
Chosen method: per-sample median of log2-transformed peptide intensities.

Rationale:
- The median is robust to the large within-protein intensity spread.
  A small number of very bright peptides (high ionization) or very
  dim peptides will not bias the protein-level estimate the way a
  mean would.
- The task says the absolute level of each protein is not evaluated,
  only its pattern across samples. The median is a consistent
  estimator of the central tendency of the peptide distribution
  within each protein, which should track the true protein
  abundance across samples.
- Log2 transformation is applied first so that the median operates
  on a scale where multiplicative ionization differences become
  additive and the distribution is approximately symmetric.
- No further sample normalization, imputation, or differential
  analysis was applied, per the task constraints.

Shared peptide handling:
- Peptides whose Proteins field contained more than one ID were
  excluded from quantification. They cannot be unambiguously
  assigned to a single protein, and including them would introduce
  bias (a peptide shared by two proteins would artificially inflate
  both).

Missing value handling:
- If a peptide is missing in a sample, it simply does not contribute
  to that sample's median (the median is computed over observed
  peptides only).
- In the rare case where no peptide for a protein is observed in a
  sample, the value is imputed with the protein's median across its
  observed samples. (In practice this was not needed: every protein
  had at least one observed peptide in every sample.)

4. OUTPUTS
----------
- protein_matrix_log2.csv: 300 proteins x 6 samples, log2 scale,
  no missing values, column order matches input.
- run_manifest.json: counts and method description.

5. CAVEATS
----------
- The median assumes peptide intensities within a protein are
  exchangeable conditional on protein abundance. If some peptides
  are systematically biased (e.g., post-translational modification
  peptides, low-charge-state peptides with different recovery),
  the median is less affected than the mean but not immune.
- Shared peptides (187) were excluded entirely. For proteins with
  few unique peptides, this reduces the effective peptide count and
  increases variance, though the median remains defined.
- The task states that samples are already normalized; no
  additional normalization was applied.
