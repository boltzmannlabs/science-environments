# Base-agent and environment-assisted comparison protocol

**Status: protocol template for implementation and task review.** No scientific comparison is established by this document. Values marked as task decisions must be frozen before a scored run; they are not environment defaults.

The purpose is to compare task outcomes and operational reliability using the same scientific case and observable resource allowances. The environment's selected behavior is advisory: it warns and records. Evaluator eligibility remains separate from whether an agent chose to continue after a warning.

## 1. Separate three validation levels

| Level | What it can establish | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synthetic software fixture | Record association, compatibility checks, warnings, accounting, recovery, and evaluator behavior using explicitly artificial values. | Molecular efficacy, prediction accuracy, live-service readiness, or independent experimental success. |
| Native execution qualification | Actual reachability of installed operations and observation coverage on the recorded Agent-Scientist version; any scientific job qualification has its own receipts. | That the environment improves agent outcomes across tasks. |
| Frozen scientific task comparison | Outcome differences on the specified cohort and endpoint under the reported design and access boundary. | Universal superiority, zero errors, prospective activity for unmeasured compounds, or independent evidence beyond the demonstrated boundary. |

Synthetic fixture values and expected answers may be public developer assets. A software evaluator test using another directory is not proof that a tested agent cannot read real final answers. A real hidden-label experiment needs a separately verified host/process/tool access boundary.

## 2. Preregister the task and comparison

Before final scoring, record the following in a versioned task protocol and preserve its digest. Do not fill unresolved scientific choices with convenient defaults.

| Required decision | Contents to freeze |
| --- | --- |
| Task and source snapshot | Task version; raw asset hashes and rights/provenance; candidate/form policy; eligible cohort and exclusion reasons; starting lead(s). |
| Scientific question | Improvement endpoints and comparators; preservation requirements; hard constraints; units/scale; compatible evidence and missing/censored-data policy. |
| Search access | Evidence visible initially or obtainable during search, permitted tools, models and versions where known, datasets, feedback and network/retrieval rules. |
| Final evidence | Measured labels or selected computational evaluator; source/model exposure review; what independence claims are and are not supportable. |
| Output | Exact submitted identities/representations and artifact roles; selection count/order if relevant; tie, duplicate, invalid-identity and incomplete-output handling. |
| Primary outcome | Task-specific primary metric and aggregation; secondary metrics; eligibility criteria; whether thresholds are justified and where. |
| Agent conditions | Base and assisted configurations, model route/version/settings, prompt and tool differences, shared starting context, environment version, and observation coverage. |
| Allowances and accounting | Separate observable units and limits, pending commitments, unknown internal work, overage/eligibility handling, and treatment of model/tool failures. |
| Repeats and analysis | Number of repeated episodes, randomization/seeds where supported, task/run ordering, baselines, inclusion/exclusion rules, and planned comparisons/uncertainty reporting. |
| Freeze and access | Submission-finalization point, evaluator identity/access, protected label location and actual accessibility checks, release of final feedback, and retention of failed runs. |

Starting-lead baseline observations must be distinguished from reference-agent and simple selection baselines. If the protocol includes random selection or a fixed single-score policy, define its access and settings before observing final outcomes. No universal baseline algorithm or number of repeats is imposed here.

## 3. Matched execution conditions

Both arms receive the same scientific task, visible starting evidence, candidate scope, and permitted scientific tools. The assisted arm receives the environment's reviewed guidance/operations; record those differences rather than adding unreported evidence or capabilities. Count additional model requests and other observable work associated with environment assistance.

Record model identity, runtime/tool catalogue revisions, settings, and seeds when available. Shared labels or method names do not prove identical model weights or service behavior. Repeat episodes with the declared design; do not invent random seeds for services that do not expose them. Record outages and ordering effects. A repeated agent run on the same cohort is another decision episode, not a new independent biological measurement.

Apply the same observable allowances and final eligibility rules. Continue to log overruns, warnings, execution failures, and incomplete episodes. The environment may allow continuation after advice while the evaluator marks the submission ineligible under the frozen task rules. Do not discard unfavorable runs or silently score only successful outputs.

## 4. Honest resource accounting

Report quantities with unit, scope, source, and whether observed, requested, estimated, or unknown. At minimum distinguish available model calls/tokens, scientific submissions, requested/returned candidates, molecule-level assessments, elapsed time, and final selection size. Account for pending/ambiguous submissions and retries without resetting their possible cost.

Remote optimization steps and returned candidate counts do not establish total service-internal oracle evaluations. Equal declared submission allowances do not prove equal computation, cost, model use, or total molecular evaluations. Present these limitations with results. Do not convert missing values into zero or make unsupported sample-efficiency claims.

## 5. Freeze submissions before final evaluation

Stop or snapshot the episode's writers, preserve exact final artifact bytes and hashes, and identify submitted candidate/form records before obtaining final-evaluator feedback. A declared finalization action alone is not evidence of immutability: verification must detect later replacement or modification.

Keep evaluator labels and any secret crosswalk outside tested-agent access. Verify files, tools, inherited context, artifacts, and permitted retrieval routes relevant to that claim. Record the tested boundary and failed/incomplete checks. Merely hiding columns in one file or asking the agent not to read them is insufficient.

Separate search/development feedback from final feedback. If final scores influence candidate revision, model choice, threshold tuning, or policy updates, label subsequent results development evaluation; obtain another eligible untouched endpoint/cohort before claiming a new final test. Public compound or label exposure through pretraining cannot be ruled out by local filesystem checks.

## 6. Score the evidence actually available

| Evidence used for final scoring | Supported result description |
| --- | --- |
| Same computational scorer available during search | Performance against the search objective. |
| Different predictor | Computational transfer/agreement, qualified by applicability, shared data, and methodological dependence. |
| Compatible protected historical assay records | Retrospective selection performance on that measured cohort/endpoint, qualified by public-data exposure. |
| New compatible measurements of frozen candidates | Prospective evidence under the separately documented assay conditions. |

For a restricted measured library, validate candidate membership and exact form before joining labels. Retain raw measurements, qualifier information, and source references in evaluator records. Unknown, unmatched, duplicate, or modified candidates receive the frozen task's eligibility outcome rather than borrowed measurements. Only declared scientifically meaningful comparisons may produce differences or aggregate metrics.

For open analogue design, absent assay data remain absent. A generated molecule cannot inherit the starting lead's assay result, a nearest neighbour's measurement, or a source series' activity. Keep experimental, predicted, and derived fields distinct.

## 7. Report outcomes and limits

Report all started episodes, their condition, final status/eligibility, selected candidates, primary and secondary outcomes, objective/constraint tradeoffs, warnings, failures, coverage gaps, and observable resource use. Keep operational reliability measures separate from chemical outcomes. Identify whether improvements are over the starting lead, an immediate parent, a fixed policy, or the base agent.

Use the preregistered aggregation and paired/task-aware comparison where the design permits it. Report repeat variability and uncertainty only to the extent the available tasks and episodes support them; repeated measurements or predictions are not automatically independent. A single target case supports a conclusion about that case, not general superiority across lead optimization.

## 8. Current implementation boundary

Synthetic evaluator and runtime checks, when implemented and executed, must be reported with their actual scope and receipts. They cannot mark the pending [Moonshot task](../tasks/covid-moonshot/README.md) scientifically complete. No repeat count, selection threshold, primary metric, real protected dataset, native episode, or base-agent scientific comparison is selected or claimed by this protocol template.
