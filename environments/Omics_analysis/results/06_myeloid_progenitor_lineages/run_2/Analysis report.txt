BONE MARROW MYELOID PROGENITORS (GSE72857) — SINGLE-CELL RNA-SEQ ANALYSIS REPORT
================================================================================

1. DATASET SUMMARY
------------------
Source: NCBI GEO accession GSE72857 (Mus musculus). Two supplementary files were
used: GSE72857_umitab.txt.gz (UMI count matrix, 27,297 genes x 10,368 cells;
cells are well IDs, e.g. W29953) and GSE72857_experimental_design.txt.gz (one row
per well, mapping each cell to its well, sequencing batch, amplification batch,
mouse, plate and sorting/genotype "Batch_desc"). Every matrix column matched a
design-table well ID exactly (no mismatches, no extra cells).

The design table shows 10,368 sorted wells, of which 108 are EMPTY wells
(Number_of_cells = 0) — these were retained in the matrix and used to estimate
the ambient/background UMI floor. The 10,260 non-empty wells come from 8
sorting/genotype conditions (Batch_desc), one mouse per condition, 27 sorting
plates, 54 amplification (cDNA) batches and 6 Illumina sequencing batches:
  - Unsorted myeloid (3,072 wells)
  - Cebpa KO (2,304) vs Cebpa control (1,152)
  - Cebpe KO (768) vs Cebpe control (384)
  - CMP CD41 (384), CMP Flt3+ Csf1r+ (1,152), CMP Irf8-GFP+ MHCII+ (1,152)

A critical structural feature: each sorting fraction was sequenced in its own
sequencing batch, so Batch_desc is fully confounded with Seq_batch_ID and with
the set of amplification batches. Amplification batches nest perfectly inside
fractions (no amp batch spans two fractions), and within a fraction cells from
one mouse share multiple amp batches. Batch handling was designed around this
(confounding means we cannot "correct away" sorting/genotype as if it were a
technical factor — see section 2).

2. QUALITY CONTROL AND BATCH HANDLING
-------------------------------------
Cell QC (applied to raw UMI counts before any transformation):
  - All 108 empty wells removed (by design flag Number_of_cells = 0).
  - Non-empty wells with < 500 total UMI removed. Justification: the empty-well
    ambient floor has a MAX of 233 UMI (p95 ~118), and the non-empty UMI
    distribution shows a low-abundance tail (p10 = 166) separated from the main
    population (p25 = 860). The <500-UMI cells have a flat, non-cell-like gene
    concentration profile (top-50-gene UMI fraction ~0.13-0.16, similar to or
    lower than the population mean), consistent with ambient RNA/poor capture
    rather than a biological population. Note this tail is enriched in Cebpa KO
    (37% <400 UMI) vs the CMP fractions (<2%): low UMI is a technical property
    of those sorts, not evidence that Cebpa KO cells are "dying", so the cut is
    set by the ambient floor rather than a per-batch percentile.
  - Result: 10,368 input -> 8,399 retained cells.
Gene QC: genes detected in >= 3 cells retained -> 14,225 genes.
No mitochondrial-gene filter was applied: the gene panel contains no mt-/* genes
(0% mitochondrial content in every cell), so such a filter would have been a
no-op. Ribosomal content (median ~10%) was monitored but not used to discard
cells.

Batch handling:
  - HVG selection was amp-batch-aware (Seurat flavor, batch_key = Amp_batch_ID)
    so that genes variable within any single fraction could enter the analysis,
    avoiding a bias toward genes variable across the (confounded) sorting
    fractions.
  - The neighbor graph was built bbknn-style: a GLOBAL k=100 kNN in 30-D PCA,
    trimmed to at most 13 neighbors per amplification batch (~50 total), then
    UMAP fuzzy-simplicial-set connectivities. This forces cross-batch mixing
    (a cell's local neighborhood cannot be monopolized by its own amp batch)
    while preserving biology. An early attempt using within-batch-only
    connectivity produced ~54 uniform clusters equal to the amp batches (total
    overcorrection) and was discarded.
  - Because sorting fraction and genotype are confounded with sequencing and
    amplification batches, integration was intentionally NOT used to merge the
    fractions: the biological question is precisely how these distinct
    progenitor populations relate. Residual batch structure that coincides with
    fraction is therefore expected and is reported per cluster below.

3. NORMALIZATION, CLUSTERING, TRAJECTORY METHODS AND PARAMETERS
---------------------------------------------------------------
  - Normalization: library-size normalization to 1e4 UMI/cell, then log1p. Raw
    integer counts kept in a layer.
  - Features: 3,000 Seurat-style highly variable genes (amp-batch-aware),
    z-score scaled (clipped at +/-10).
  - Dimensionality reduction: PCA, 50 components computed, 30 used for the
    graph; UMAP (n_neighbors=50, min_dist=0.3) on the batch-aware graph.
  - Clustering: Leiden (igraph flavor, n_iterations=2) on the batch-aware
    kNN graph. Resolution 0.7 was selected from a range (0.3/0.5/0.7/1.0):
    0.3 merged lineages that have distinct marker programs; 1.0 began splitting
    clusters along batch/genotype lines rather than transcriptional state.
    12 clusters resulted.
  - Markers: two-sample t-test per cluster vs the rest, BH-adjusted p-values,
    top 20 genes per cluster (score = t-statistic magnitude, log2 fold change
    with pseudocount).
  - Relationships: PAGA (partitioned graph analysis) on the same batch-aware
    graph, 12 cluster states.
  - Pseudotime: diffusion pseudotime implemented directly on the batch-aware
    connectivity graph (random-walk transition matrix, 7 non-trivial diffusion
    eigen-coordinates, Euclidean diffusion distance from a Cd34+ CMP root
    cell), normalized to [0,1]. Each cell also carries a lineage/branch label
    derived from its cluster.

4. SUBPOPULATIONS, FATES AND EVIDENCE
-------------------------------------
12 clusters were identified (n = cells; top markers with log2FC):

  cl 10  common myeloid progenitor (CMP), n=568. Cd34 (2.79), H2-Aa, Eef1a1.
         Enriched in the CMP Irf8-GFP+ MHCII+ sort (511/568) and CMP Flt3+
         Csf1r+ sort. The most progenitor-like state (Cd34+, low lineage
         commitment); root of the pseudotime.
  cl 2   multipotent myeloid progenitor, n=110. Gata2 (4.07), Srgn, Lmo4,
         Lgals1, Ly6e. Small, from unsorted myeloid and Cebpe KO. Gata2 is a
         hematopoietic progenitor factor; this state sits between the CMP and
         the erythroid branch on PAGA (cl2<->cl1 weight 0.87).
  cl 9   granulocyte-macrophage progenitor (GMP), n=153. Plac8 (4.60), B2m,
         Atpase6. From CMP Flt3+ Csf1r+ (119/153) — the Flt3+Csf1r+ gate is
         the classical GMP marker combination. Strongest PAGA edge to the
         immature granulocyte cluster (cl9<->cl4 = 1.00).
  cl 4   immature granulocyte / progranulocyte, n=1,445. Mpo (2.23), Prtn3
         (2.30), Ramp1 (2.16), H2afy, Lgals1. From CMP Flt3+ Csf1r+
         (993/1,445). Granulocytic enzymes present but at lower fold change
         than mature states; the committed granulocytic entry point.
  cl 3   mature neutrophil, n=1,484. Prtn3 (4.13), Mpo (3.41), Ctsg (3.73),
         Elane (5.07), Calr. Mostly unsorted myeloid (1,023/1,484) plus Cebpa
         and Cebpe controls. The canonical mature neutrophil granule program.
  cl 11  neutrophil-like, Cebpe-KO-enriched, n=266. Elane (6.25), Prtn3 (4.33),
         Mpo (3.53), Ly6c2 (5.34), Ctsg, Hp. 260/266 are Cebpe KO and the
         cluster is concentrated in four Cebpe-KO amplification batches
         (AB283/284/291/292). It is transcriptionally a neutrophil state but
         its separation from cl 3 is reinforced by genotype/batch, so treat its
         distinctness with caution (see limitations).
  cl 1   early erythroid, n=1,369. Apoe (3.19), Car1 (2.15), Car2 (1.73),
         Vamp5, S100a10. Enriched in Cebpa KO (869/1,369): Cebpa-deficient
         progenitors fail to commit to granulopoiesis and skew erythroid,
         which is exactly what this cluster shows. Car1/Car2 (erythrocyte
         carboxypeptidases) are established erythroid markers.
  cl 5   mature erythroid, n=1,927. Car2 (4.59), Blvrb (4.31), Car1 (4.90),
         Klf1 (4.41), Fam132a, Mt2. Klf1 is the erythroid master transcription
         factor. The largest single cluster; spans unsorted and both Cebpa
         conditions.
  cl 6   ribosome-rich late erythroid/megakaryocyte, n=165. Ribosomal proteins
         (Rps5, Rpl14, Rpl13a) dominate, with Blvrb (2.90), Aqp1 (2.97), Ftl1
         among non-ribosomal markers. Aqp1 and Blvrb mark late erythroid /
         megakaryocytic cells; the ribosomal dominance reflects the
         translational burst of terminal erythroid maturation. Enriched in
         Cebpa control (151/165). PAGA links it to both erythroid clusters
         (cl1<->cl6 0.72, cl5<->cl6 0.74) and the megakaryocyte cluster.
  cl 0   megakaryocyte, n=382. Nrgn (4.54), Itga2b (6.29), Pf4 (8.29),
         Serpina3f, Gpr56, Cdcrel-1. Itga2b (CD41) and Pf4 are specific
         megakaryocyte/platelet markers. 307/382 from the CMP CD41 sort — the
         same CD41 (Itga2b) marker used to sort the progenitors is highly
         expressed here, consistent with this being the mature CD41 lineage
         output.
  cl 7   MHC-low myeloid precursor, n=59. Ccl5 (9.91), H2-K1 (2.28), Cd52,
         Tcb. Small; from unsorted and the Irf8-GFP+ MHCII+ sort. A
         low-MHCII myeloid state bridging the CMP and the MHCII+ DC cluster on
         PAGA.
  cl 8   MHCII+ dendritic cell, n=471. Cd74 (9.55), H2-Aa (9.06), Cst3 (4.74),
         H2-Ab1 (7.87), Actb, Lsp1. MHC class II (and HLA-DM homolog Cd74)
         defines this population; 413/471 from the CMP Irf8-GFP+ MHCII+ sort,
         the fraction explicitly enriched for Irf8 (the DC-lineage factor).

Differentiation fates identified:
  - Granulocytic/neutrophil: cl9 (GMP) -> cl4 (immature) -> cl3 (mature), with
    the cl11 Cebpe-KO branch.
  - Erythroid: cl2 (progenitor) -> cl1 (early) -> cl5 (mature) / cl6 (late,
    ribosome-rich).
  - Megakaryocytic: cl0 (megakaryocyte), branching from the early erythroid
    state (shared CD41/Itga2b and Blvrb programs, as expected for the
    megakaryocyte-erythroid progenitor compartment).
  - Dendritic (MHCII+): cl10 (Cd34+ CMP) -> cl7 (MHC-low) -> cl8 (MHCII+ DC).
  - A monocytic fate is not cleanly resolved: no cluster shows a strong
    monocyte program (e.g. Csf1r, Ly6c+ monocytic signature) distinct from the
    states above; Csf1r appears only as a sort marker, not a strong cluster
    marker. Monocytic commitment, if present, is either minor or not captured
    at this QC/depth.

Relationships (PAGA, edge weights): the graph is organized around the cl10
Cd34+ CMP and the cl9 GMP as shared myeloid entry points. Granulocytic axis:
cl10/cl9 -> cl4 (w=1.00) -> cl3 (w=0.62) <-> cl11 (w=0.62). Erythroid axis:
cl2 -> cl1 (w=0.87) -> cl5 (w=0.28) and cl6 (w=0.72-0.74); cl0 attaches to
cl1 (w=0.51) and cl6 (w=0.49). DC axis: cl10 -> cl7 (w=0.17) -> cl8
(weak direct edge; cl8 mainly contacts cl10 w=0.06 and cl4 w=0.06). The
progenitor clusters (cl10, cl9, cl2) form the hub from which all branches
emanate.

Pseudotime (diffusion distance from the cl10 root, 0=most progenitor-like):
mean values rise monotonically from progenitors (cl10 0.07) through committed
states (cl4/cl9 ~0.11, cl1 ~0.13) to the most differentiated/terminal states
(cl0 0.24, cl8 0.26). This is a global differentiation-depth axis, not a
single linear trajectory: the dataset is cross-sectional (distinct sorted
fractions and genotypes, not a time course), so "pseudotime" here orders states
by inferred differentiation distance from the progenitor pool.

5. UNCERTAINTIES AND LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------
  - Cross-sectional, mixed-fraction design: the fractions (CD41, Flt3+Csf1r+,
    Irf8-GFP+MHCII+, unsorted, Cebpa +/-, Cebpe +/-) were sorted from one
    mouse each and sequenced in separate batches. There is no time course and
    no within-mouse mixing, so trajectories are inferred from transcriptional
    continuity, not observed transitions. Pseudotime is a differentiation-depth
    proxy and should not be read as a clock.
  - Confounded batch/genotype/fraction: sorting fraction is inseparable from
    sequencing and amplification batch. Batch-aware kNN trimming reduces
    amplification-batch structure but cannot and should not "correct"
    biological differences between fractions. Residual structure aligned with
    fraction therefore remains; cluster identities lean on marker biology and
    are annotated accordingly.
  - cl11 (Cebpe-KO neutrophil-like) is concentrated in four Cebpe-KO amp
    batches; its separation from cl3 may reflect a real Cebpe-KO-specific
    transcriptional state, a residual batch effect, or both. It is labeled as
    such and should be interpreted cautiously.
  - cl6 is defined by ribosomal dominance; its terminal erythroid/megakaryocyte
    identity rests on Aqp1/Blvrb/Ftl1 rather than a broad erythroid program.
    It could partly reflect a high-translation cell cycle / maturation state.
  - No doublet call was made (no 3' doublet index in the data); the highest
    UMI cells (max ~26,000) were retained, so multiplets, if present, are not
    removed.
  - Marker tests are two-sample t-tests on log-normalized HVG counts; for rare
    genes or small clusters (cl2 n=110, cl7 n=59) scores are less stable.
  - The monocytic branch is not resolved; absence of a cluster marker does not
    prove absence of monocytes.
  - Gene names with aliases ("Gene;Alias") were collapsed to the primary name
    (verified unique); no annotation database was used, so labels rely on
    standard mouse gene knowledge (e.g. Klf1=erythroid TF, Itga2b=CD41).
  - 2015-era UMI protocol: total UMI counts (median ~2,100) are low by modern
    standards; rare populations and subtle state differences may be underpowered.
