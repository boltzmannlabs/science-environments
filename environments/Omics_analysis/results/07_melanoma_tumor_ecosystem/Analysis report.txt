GSE72056 METASTATIC MELANOMA: independent exploratory analysis
Source: https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE72nnn/GSE72056/suppl/GSE72056_melanoma_single_cell_revised_v2.txt.gz
The two supplied malignant/cell-type annotation rows were excluded during CSV parsing (skiprows=[2,3]); only the tumor row and gene values were used. No tutorial, published answer, or reference labels were consulted.

DATA AND QC. 4645 cells, 21924 genes expressed in at least five cells, 4645 retained from 19 tumors. Input range 0–15.923; most positive values are fractional and inverse transformed 2^x−1 totals have median ≈99,999 per cell, establishing log2(TPM/10+1), not raw counts. No library-size renormalization, no second log transform; gene filtering ≥5 cells, cell detection ≥500 genes, finite values, estimated mitochondrial fraction <30%. No genes start MT- (the similarly named INMT-FAM188B is NOT mitochondrial), so mitochondrial exclusion was inoperative; all cells retained. Data already prefiltered at source: minimum input detected genes 1370, hence this is not raw-cell QC. No count-based doublet detection can be performed reliably from TPM. Duplicated gene symbols were made unique within AnnData.

CELL ANNOTATION. Leiden resolution 0.65 on 3000 log-expression HVGs (ribosomal/mitochondrial/immune receptor genes excluded from embedding), 45 PC PCA, 35 PCs for 20-neighbor graph; UMAP only visualizes, does not define types. Malignancy is inferred from isolated tumor-specific expression islands plus SOX10/MITF/PMEL/MLANA/TYR/DCT/MIA/PRAME signal, and low leukocyte PTPRC/TCR. This is NOT a copy-number-based validation and poorly differentiated tumor cells may be missed. T cells express CD3D/CD3E/TRAC; B cells CD79A/MS4A1/BANK1; macrophages LYZ/C1QA/CD68; NK cells GNLY/NKG7/KLRD1 without strong TCR; fibroblasts COL1A1/COL1A2/DCN; endothelial cells PECAM1/VWF/CLDN5; plasma cells MZB1/XBP1; dendritic cells IL3RA/PLD4/IRF7. Mixed-lineage or weak-marker cells are unresolved. Cluster 14 includes high PTPRC, TCR and apparent melanoma antigens: possible ambient RNA/doublets, not automatically malignant.

Malignant cells are strongly tumor-associated whereas non-malignant neighborhoods share phenotype across tumors; these descriptive proportions are not hypothesis-test p-values and are sensitive to clustering, sampling and batch/tumor confounding.

MARKERS. One-vs-rest Wilcoxon rank scores with BH-adjusted p values are exploratory cell-level association tests and not patient-level differential-expression inference. log2 fold change compares arithmetic mean inverse-transformed TPM/10+1, with a +0.1 pseudocount in TPM/10 units. top positive genes up to 60 per cell type; different tumors contribute unequal cell counts.

MALIGNANT PROGRAMS. For malignant cells, within each tumor subtract per-gene mean on log2 expression. Require gene expression and variability in at least three tumors with >=30 malignant cells, exclude ribosomal and receptor genes, retain the 3000 highest median within-tumor variance genes; divide by (overall SD+0.5), clip at ±4, fit 10-component FastICA (seed 41). Select six coherent, interpretable component directions; two opposing signs of one component are different endpoints of a differentiation/immune axis, not independent factors. Gene score is signed component weight divided by its maximum positive weight; top 100 genes per chosen direction. This selection is exploratory, not a rank-validation or proof of a fixed program count. Top-quintile cell prevalence verifies that each is represented in multiple tumors.

LIMITATIONS. TPM and processed pre-filtered cells preclude UMI-based depth/doublet inference, mitochondrial QC, and direct genome-ordered CNV detection. Tumor identity is also a technical batch and cannot distinguish genetic from batch structure. Cell-level Wilcoxon p values are anti-conservative across within-patient cells. Mixed-lineage signal can be ambient RNA or doublets. Labels and NMF factors require independent validation (CNV/genotype, protein or spatial assays). UMAP distances do not equal statistical distances.
