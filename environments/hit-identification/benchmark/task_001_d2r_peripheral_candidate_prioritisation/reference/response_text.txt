5. The manuscript highlights Compound #381 as the lead candidate, but no comparative discussion is provided regarding its synthetic feasibility, predicted toxicity, or metabolic stability relative to existing drugs like domperidone or risperidone.

Response: 
In the revised analysis, the 100 ns MD simulations showed that Compound #128 exhibited the most stable ligand RMSD profile among the candidate compounds, whereas Compound #381 retained favorable BBB restriction and comparatively favorable hERG-related prediction. Therefore, we revised the manuscript to present Compounds #128 and #381 as prioritized candidates rather than emphasizing Compound #381 alone.

We also added Table 5 to compare #128 and #381 with risperidone and domperidone in terms of drug-likeness, absorption, hERG-related cardiotoxicity, CYP-related metabolic interaction predictions, and representative off-target receptor docking. Both candidates satisfied major drug-likeness criteria and showed high predicted gastrointestinal absorption. Compound #128 had a synthetic accessibility score comparable to risperidone, while #381 showed moderate synthetic feasibility. Although #381 showed a lower hERG-10 μM probability than risperidone and domperidone, both candidates retained predicted hERG and CYP-related liabilities that require experimental validation. Representative off-target docking further indicated that #128 and #381 did not uniformly show stronger binding to 5-HT2A, H1, and α1B-adrenergic receptors than the reference drugs.

The Results, Discussion, and Conclusion were revised accordingly to clarify the complementary prioritization of #128 and #381 and to emphasize the need for future experimental validation.

6. The docking validation strategy is insufficiently described. Re-docking of risperidone with RMSD comparison to the crystal pose should be reported quantitatively to validate the docking protocol reliability.

Response: 
We performed re-docking validation using the co-crystallized risperidone ligand (8NU) extracted from the D2R crystal structure (PDB ID: 6CM4). The initially prepared risperidone structure generated from SMILES did not sufficiently reproduce the crystallographic pose; therefore, the co-crystallized 8NU ligand was used as a more appropriate reference for docking validation and MD comparison.

The re-docked 8NU pose closely overlapped with the crystal pose, yielding a heavy-atom RMSD of 0.458 Å. The docking score of the 8NU reference was −11.8 kcal/mol, slightly different from the previously used SMILES-derived risperidone value of −11.9 kcal/mol. We therefore revised the docking selection criterion to compounds with binding affinities more favorable than the 8NU reference. This change did not affect the selected candidates or downstream analyses, because all prioritized candidates showed docking scores better than −11.8 kcal/mol.

In addition, use of the co-crystallized 8NU ligand provided a more stable reference system in the 100 ns MD simulation, as reflected by its stable ligand RMSD trajectory. Thus, the revised 8NU-based reference improved both the docking validation and the dynamic stability benchmark for comparing the candidate compounds. The Methods, Results, and Fig. 3 have been revised accordingly.
